![My Board Game Collection](./collection/collection-cover.jpg "Board Game Collection")

A different focus from discussing specific concepts within a board game. It's more of a high-level conversation that starts with building a collection and then narrows down to selecting a game to play. Without a collection, you won't have any games to choose from. Right?

I've been in the board gaming hobby for nearly two years, and while my collection is still growing, I currently own 11 games, with most all of them including expansions, with the exceptions of Terraforming Mars and Europa Universalis. Dam! I still have to learn and play Europa Universalis!

## Started in 2023

I only started back in Jan of 2023. Maybe by the time you discover this article, situations could have changed. As of now (August 2024), I am still fresh in the hobby. My knowledge of board games is extensive, and even with a relatively small collection, I can still discuss board games in general, as I've played many other games with my gaming group.

## Financial Priorities

Money! We all wish we had tons of it so we could buy every game we come across. But let’s be honest—we all have other priorities in our lives.

With prices on the rise, especially when comparing to the United States, game prices here in Quebec, Canada, are noticeably higher.

## Selective with Board Games

First few months when I got into the hobby, I was buying games often and exploring. Sometimes I didn't even know what I was buying. My wife and kids questioned about this and didn’t understand.

Fast forward today, it’s great I explored and know what games I want in my collection. I’m not interested in buying a game that has the same mechanics but a different theme. For example if I have Wingspan why have Wyrmspan? Gloomheaven why Frostheaven? At one point I was interested in Halls of Hegra, which is a solo war game, surviving the German attacks. Since having Robinson Crusoe do I need another survival game? It’s still in my list but right now, not really motivated to play it.

## Solo Player

For me to purchase a game, it needs to have a strong solo mode. I've come across several interesting games, but if they lack solo, it;s off my list. I primarily play games solo, and while many games offer solo modes, not all of them resonate with me. For example, after about 15 plays, with Lost Ruins of Arnak, it no longer clicked for me, and since I didn't have others to play with, I decided to traded it for Mage Knight.

The solo mode in Terraforming Mars isn't particularly strong. It helped me learn the game, but eventually, it started to feel stale. However, with a new Automa solo mode expansion on the horizon, I'm keeping it in my collection and plan to give it more plays. So why look for another game when this one has new potential?

## Recycle and Replay Games

I enjoy replaying my games frequently. More experienced and knowledgeable, I choose board games that offer substantial content and high replayability. When I'm eager to add a new game to my collection, I review my existing games to determine which one can be let go. I only keep games that I can't stop thinking about, knowing I'll eventually want to play again. I don't have a "shelf of shame" filled with unplayed games. If I don't want to get rid of any, well I will squeeze it in, expecptions will be made :)

I find it challenging to understand how people manage large game collections, as I'm not sure how often I'd get to play them. If I didn't regularly recycle my games, I think my collection would have grown to 20 games with a shelf of shame on the side.

## Enjoy the Expansions

![Expansions](./collection/expansion-collection.jpg "Expansions")

Other than buying new games, I lean towards investing in expansions. Not sure if that can be considered an extra game in your collection or grouping them together as one game. If I would count them separately then yes I would have grown to 20 games.

Expansions are another way of replaying games or bringing them back to life. There are some really good expansions which enhance the game in different aspects. For example ‘Architects of the West Kingdom’. The AI became stale and not challenging. I did some research and decided to add the Works of Wonder, which totally enhanced my playing solo experience which stayed in my collection.

## Conclusion

I hope this gives you some ideas on how to manage your own collection or if you're curious about mine. One guideline I’ve heard is that for every game that comes in, another has to go out. Some hobbyists refer to this as the 'not more than (x number)' rule.

One important point is to focus on enjoying the games you have. Having a larger collection doesn't necessarily make someone a better hobbyist; it might even lead to confusion.

Personally, when I discover a game I really want, I dive deep into watching videos and reading about it. This often makes me more knowledgeable about the game, even before I own it, rather than just letting it sit in my shelf of shame.

Happy Gaming!
